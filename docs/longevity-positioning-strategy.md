# HSW longevity positioning and communication strategy

Draft for discussion — 22 September 2026. Strategy only; no app copy, advertisements, budgets or publication changed.

## Strategic decision

Campaign platform: **Age is not just a number.**

Supporting message: **Understand what matters for your health—and what to do next.**

Emotional territory: having the capacity to keep doing the things that make life worthwhile. Show everyday strength, concentration, meaningful work, family time, movement and travel. Use curiosity and agency rather than fear of ageing.

The phrase alone is broad and unlikely to distinguish the brand. HSW earns it through a recognisable sequence: a useful starting assessment, clinical interpretation, a written personal plan, and a return to agreed measures over time. Treat this as a positioning hypothesis, not a proven unique market advantage; this draft is grounded in HSW's offer and app, not a completed competitor audit.

Commercial objective: attract people suited to an assessment and continuing care, then convert appropriate consultations into paid pathways. A completed quiz or an alarming age score is not the business outcome.

## Recommended opening: start with their question

Lead the app with a question about the person's experience, then introduce **Age is not just a number** as the campaign idea that connects their answer to a useful next step. This is the recommended first creative direction to test against the headline-led opening. It is a hypothesis about relevance, not a claim that question-led pages always convert better.

Suggested first screen:

> What changes have you noticed as you've got older?

Supporting text: Choose what you would most like to understand. Changes can have many causes; this assessment cannot establish the reason.

Offer these simple choices, with multiple selection where useful:

- My energy through the day.
- My concentration or mental clarity.
- My sleep and how rested I feel.
- My weight or body composition.
- My strength, stamina or recovery.
- No particular concern - I want to plan ahead.

Make the last choice exclusive of the others and offer “Something else” or “Prefer not to say”. Do not force the person to report a problem. These are proposed copy choices; map them carefully to existing concern categories before implementation. The current model should not add years because somebody selects a symptom or concern.

Follow with one short question about the outcome they value:

> What would you most like your health to support?

Examples: staying active; being present with family; concentrating at work; enjoying travel and hobbies; understanding my health; another priority. This gives the consultation personal context without promising the service will deliver a particular result.

Then introduce the campaign:

> Age is not just a number. Your experience matters too.

> Start with your everyday habits. Receive a lifestyle-based report and explore what may be worth discussing with a clinician.

Button: **Continue to my assessment**. Keep the estimate explanation and approximate completion time visible. Integrate these questions into the existing goal/concern steps, rather than adding a second questionnaire before the current one. Show the time estimate only after timing the revised flow.

For organic video, use questions as educational hooks: “Why can changes in energy, sleep or recovery have different explanations?” Dr Ahmad can explain how assessment helps identify appropriate next steps. Avoid presenting ageing as the established cause of every change.

For paid creative, start more broadly: **“What matters most to you as you get older?”** Examples can include staying active, thinking clearly and planning ahead. Keep symptom-specific self-report questions inside the voluntary assessment rather than writing ads that presume the viewer has a health problem. Check the final creative against the platform's current rules before launch.

The report should reflect their chosen priority in plain language, explain any relevant pathway and offer a discussion. Persistent or new symptoms need appropriate clinical assessment; an age score should never be used to dismiss or diagnose them.

Compare the question-led and headline-led openings using completion, qualified consultation bookings and attendance. Keep the rest of the journey constant so the result is interpretable.

## Positioning

Recommended positioning statement:

> For adults who want a clearer understanding of their health as they age, Harley Street Medical Wellness connects personal priorities, relevant assessment and testing with an individual care plan and continuing review.

Public-facing service descriptor: **Personalised longevity care.**

App descriptor: **Your lifestyle age starting point.**

Keep the HSW name prominent. The app is the accessible first step into the service, rather than a separate brand with a separate promise. Active Years remains the named healthy-ageing pathway; Clear Focus and Metabolic Momentum are relevant entry points when those concerns arise.

The service promise is a considered process and understandable next steps. Improving energy, strength or metabolic health may be individual goals, but they are not guaranteed results of the questionnaire, a testing package or an infusion.

## Audience to start with

Working acquisition hypothesis: adults roughly 40–60 in the clinic catchment areas who value their future health, can consider private care and want a more organised way to act. This is an initial creative audience, not a finding from conversion data and not a medical eligibility criterion.

Three motivations can share the same service:

| Motivation | What they are asking | Message | Relevant next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stay capable | How can I keep doing what matters to me? | Put your health priorities into a plan. | Active Years assessment discussion |
| Understand a change | Where should I start with energy, focus or weight concerns? | Start with the concern. Explore an appropriate assessment. | Clear Focus or Metabolic Momentum where relevant |
| Make sense of information | Which of these tests and recommendations actually matter to me? | Information becomes useful when someone explains how it relates to your care. | Results interpretation and a written plan |

Start with capability and clarity as the main campaign. Use symptom-specific content as separate entry routes. Avoid creative that assumes a viewer has a disease, is deteriorating, or has an abnormal result.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Attention:** Age is not just a number.
2. **Personal relevance:** Your birthday tells one part of the story. Your habits, health history and priorities deserve attention too.
3. **Accessible action:** Start with a free lifestyle assessment and see which habits stand out in your report.
4. **Interpretation:** Your report is a starting point for discussion, not a biological-age measurement or diagnosis.
5. **Clinical value:** A clinician can review your priorities, discuss relevant investigations and explain appropriate next steps.
6. **Continuing value:** Agree what to review, when to review it and how the plan should change.

Lead with this sequence. Explain individual treatments after relevance and suitability are established. Epigenetic testing is one possible information source, not the campaign's universal answer.

## The conversion journey

| Stage | Job of the communication | Suggested message / action |
|---|---|---|
| Organic reel or paid creative | Create curiosity about care beyond a birthday | Age is not just a number. Start with your everyday habits. |
| App landing page | Explain exactly what the visitor receives | A free lifestyle assessment, a personal report and a clearer starting point. |
| Questions | Make reflection easy | Simple questions; honest progress; explain why information is requested. |
| Report | Give useful value immediately | Show habits behind the estimate, helpful next steps and uncertainty alongside the result. |
| What-if simulator | Explore how the model responds to answers | Show how this estimate changes under a different scenario; do not imply promised years gained. |
| Protocol video | Make the care process tangible | Watch what assessment, a plan and review can involve. |
| Consultation | Explain why a conversation is worthwhile | Discuss your report and what is worth exploring next. |
| Paid assessment / plan | Make the offer concrete | Scope, selected investigations, deliverables, clinician, fees and review arrangements. |
| Continuing review | Give ongoing care a clear purpose | Return to agreed health measures and meaningful daily-life goals. |

Keep useful report information available even when someone is not ready to purchase. Current code suppresses the clinic offer after a negative £500 investment answer; keep that behaviour unchanged for now, but test later whether a softer readiness question improves appropriate bookings. Any pricing promise must match a confirmed offer.

Suggested report CTA: **Discuss my report**. Use a named-doctor booking CTA only when that doctor is confirmed to take the booked appointment.

## Content and visual communication

Build four recurring themes, each resolving a specific question:

- **Beyond the birthday:** What else belongs in a conversation about ageing and health? Hook: “What should you know about your health beyond your age?”
- **Make the report useful:** What do the estimate and its individual factors mean? Demonstrate an explicitly illustrative report without presenting its score as a measured patient result.
- **Explain the care:** What happens after someone books? Use the existing Clear Focus and Metabolic Momentum films, clinician explanation, and verified consultation/results/planning footage when available.
- **Explain testing clearly:** How do inherited DNA information, epigenetic age estimates and routine clinical tests differ? Make their different roles and limits understandable.

Use Dr Ahmad's alternate saved looks for variety, preserving his identity. Retain the user's preference against on-screen avatar labels in these films. Avoid implying a generated demonstration is a recording of actual patient care. Do not use supplier branding. Prioritise footage of listening, explaining and reviewing; unrelated procedure footage does not prove the service's benefits.

Brand behaviour: calm, specific, clinically informed and accessible. Navy/gold can signal a premium service; clear explanations and a transparent offer must supply the substance. Avoid countdown clocks, dramatic damage graphics and a red “older than you think” reveal.

## Initial creative hypotheses

| Concept | Opening copy | Payoff | What to compare |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beyond the birthday | Age is not just a number. What else should you understand? | Show the report's habits and next steps. | Assessment starts and qualified attended consultations |
| Life ahead | What do you want to keep doing in the years ahead? | Connect personal priorities with assessment and review. | Completion and consultation attendance |
| From score to plan | A health score raises questions. A conversation helps you decide what to do next. | Show report → explanation → care plan. | Report-to-booking conversion and suitability |

Keep the destination, offer and audience consistent for the first comparison. Do not change the headline, audience, price and consultation promise at once. These are hypotheses to test, not predicted winners. No budget or conversion benchmark is assumed.

Organic content can explain one question per post and build familiarity with Dr Ahmad. Paid creative should carry the same message as its destination and give a precise next action. Prepare follow-up communications only for an appropriately consented audience; this strategy does not send any messages or launch a campaign.

## Measurement

Track a defined journey: assessment started → completed → report viewed → relevant video played → booking started → booked → attended → assessed suitable → paid plan started. Add care retention later where clinically and commercially meaningful.

Use cost per qualified attended consultation and appropriate paid-plan conversion as the main commercial measures. Video retention, completion rate and cost per lead are diagnostic measures. Define a qualified consultation before comparison; lack of a treatment indication is not a sales failure to overcome.

Use aggregate behavioural events. Keep symptoms, answers, age estimates and test results out of advertising pixels, event names, URLs and audience labels. Maintain the distinction between operational clinical records and advertising measurement.

## App alignment needed before a campaign launch

These are recommendations for a separate implementation pass, not changes made by this document:

1. Replace the landing headline “We Measure Before We Recommend Anything” for the questionnaire experience; it can be read as promising measurement at that stage. Introduce the campaign line and precise assessment description.
2. Label the output consistently as a lifestyle-based estimate. The current population-association model is not established here as an individually validated biological-age predictor. Do not promote an exact “true age”, rate of ageing or lifespan prediction.
3. Reframe “Only measurement confirms it” in the report. Tests add information; they do not validate this questionnaire's numerical estimate or establish one definitive biological age.
4. Review the epigenetic copy's “within about five years” phrasing. Average prediction error from a study is not a personal confidence interval or repeat-test precision. Avoid giving the impression that a five-year shift proves successful treatment.
5. Verify the consultation host, duration, fee and availability before promising “10 minutes with Dr Ahmad”. The local configuration names him, while the booking URL is labelled wellness-consultant-1 and the live website says the team confirms the appointment clinician. The URL label alone cannot resolve who takes the call.
6. Make privacy information accessible before collecting contact details. The app's central privacy URL is currently empty. Separate necessary report communications from optional marketing preferences.
7. Confirm the actual paid offer: what assessment and interpretation include, what costs extra, and what follow-up is available. Do not invent an all-inclusive longevity package or guarantee a number of years of improvement.
