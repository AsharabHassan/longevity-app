## 0. Executive summary

1. **Stem cells.** Almost any "stem cell" product a private clinic could realistically administer for longevity (expanded MSCs, umbilical/Wharton's jelly MSCs, adipose SVF given IV or into joints, exosomes by injection) is, in UK law, either an unlicensed advanced therapy medicinal product (ATMP) or an unlicensed medicine. It can only be supplied lawfully through the hospital-exemption or "specials" routes, both of which require an **MHRA-licensed manufacturer**, plus **HTA licensing** for procurement/testing of the starting tissue. MHRA's own guidance says **unlicensed medicines "should not be mentioned"** on a clinic website and cannot be called "clinically proven" [V]. There is **no RCT evidence that IV stem cells slow ageing**; the best trial (Longeveron, laromestrocel/lomecel-B, frailty) **missed its primary endpoint at the pre-specified 6-month timepoint** (p=0.0635) and only reached significance at month 9 [V]. Conclusion: not in ads, not in the quiz result, not in the chatbot; consultation-only, and only if the supply chain is licensed.
2. **Peptides.** The owner's instinct is right. BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, sermorelin, epitalon, thymosin alpha-1 and MOTS-c have no UK marketing authorisation; advertising them breaches HMR 2012 reg. 279 [V]; licensed GLP-1s are POMs and advertising them breaches reg. 284 [V] and CAP 12.12, and the ASA now treats even "GLP-1", "weight loss injection" and an unbranded pen image as a POM reference [V]. MHRA opened an inquiry into UK peptide clinics in April 2026 after a Guardian investigation [V, secondary]. None of the "longevity" peptides has an adequately powered human RCT for a longevity or recovery outcome.
3. **Epigenetic testing.** This is the one service of the three that can be advertised - carefully. It is a lab measurement, not a medicine. The defensible claim is "we measure DNA-methylation markers of ageing in a blood sample"; the indefensible claims are "your true age", "clinically proven", and "we will reverse it by N years". New 2026 data show that even clocks with excellent *technical* reliability (ICC > 0.9) have only moderate *biological* day-to-day reliability (ICC ~0.4-0.7), with DunedinPACE and GrimAge at the lower end [V]. A single before/after pair in one patient cannot prove reversal. It is special-category data under UK GDPR and, if sent to a US lab, a restricted transfer.
3a. **Muhdo (the clinic's confirmed vendor).** Saliva sample, Illumina EPIC array, unnamed ISO 17025 lab (ISO 15189/UKAS not verified) [V]. The "peer reviewed" MethylPace basis is a real paper (Collins, Brown, Chung, *DNA* (MDPI) 2025) but **two of three authors are Muhdo employees, the data are Muhdo's, and it is a 10-CpG predictor of chronological age with r = 0.80 and mean absolute error ~5.5 years** - no outcome validation, no test-retest data in the abstract, no independent replication found [V]. **MethylPace is not DunedinPACE.** I found no published validation for Eye/Hearing/Memory Age or EpiVitality. "Gold standard" is unsupportable. The DNA panel's disease and trait calls (autism, intelligence, lupus, fibromyalgia, coeliac, T2DM, prostate) are the clinic's biggest exposure: BSGM/RCGP advise clinicians not to interpret reports from non-accredited labs [V]. Recommendation: market only the age *estimate*, with its error stated; suppress or disclaim the rest; pair with blood-based PhenoAge and/or an array-based blood clock before saying anything about change; treat retest differences under ~5 years as within test variation. See C0.
4. I found **no ASA ruling that names stem cell therapy, peptide therapy, or an epigenetic/biological-age test**. I searched repeatedly. The nearest rulings are 111 Skin (exosome cosmetic, 13 May 2026), DNAfit (DNA test, 31 March 2021), Beiersdorf (years-younger claim, 29 April 2026, in the earlier report) and the 2025-26 weight-loss POM rulings. Absence of a ruling is not clearance: the governing rules (CAP 3.1, 3.7, 12.1, 12.11, 12.12; HMR regs 279 and 284) are unambiguous.

## A. STEM CELLS

### A1. Regulatory framework

**What an ATMP is.** MHRA: "An advanced therapy medicinal product (ATMP) is a medicinal product which is either: a gene therapy medicinal product, a somatic cell therapy medicinal product, a tissue engineered product." Page last updated 11 September 2026. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/advanced-therapy-medicinal-products-regulation-and-licensing-in-uk **[V]**

**Substantial manipulation / non-homologous use.** Cells become a somatic-cell-therapy or tissue-engineered medicinal product when they (a) have been **substantially manipulated**, or (b) are **not intended to be used for the same essential function** in the recipient as in the donor. The list of manipulations that are *not* substantial (cutting, grinding, shaping, centrifugation, soaking in antibiotic solutions, sterilisation, irradiation, cell separation/concentration/purification, filtering, lyophilisation, freezing, cryopreservation, vitrification) comes from Annex I of Regulation (EC) 1394/2007, retained in UK law via the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 **[R - the MHRA page I opened did not reproduce this text; confirm against the retained Regulation or ask MHRA]**. Practical consequences **[R]**:
- **Culture-expanded MSCs** (from any source): substantially manipulated -> ATMP.
- **Adipose SVF obtained by enzymatic (collagenase) digestion**: regarded by the EMA Committee for Advanced Therapies as substantial manipulation -> ATMP. Mechanically processed fat (e.g. Lipogems-type) used as a fat graft for a structural/cushioning function is arguable as non-ATMP; the *same material* given **intravenously or for a systemic/anti-ageing purpose is non-homologous use -> ATMP**. Not verified against a UK-specific MHRA classification decision.
- **Umbilical cord / Wharton's jelly MSC products**: allogeneic, almost always expanded, and used for a non-homologous purpose -> ATMP. There is **no such product with a UK marketing authorisation** **[R]**. Imported vials sold to clinics are unlicensed medicines; import needs an MHRA import notification by a licensed wholesaler/specials importer, and import of tissues/cells for human application needs an HTA import licence.
- **Exosomes / secretome**: not cells, so not ATMPs, but when injected or infused for a physiological effect they are **medicinal products by function**, and none is licensed. Trade and clinical press report the MHRA position that injected exosomes are medicinal products that cannot lawfully be used in aesthetic procedures, and that human-derived material is prohibited in cosmetics (retained Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009, Annex II entry 416 **[R]**); a 5 News investigation found roughly 1 in 5 "exosome facial" providers using human-derived product. https://www.path.cam.ac.uk/news/beauty-clinics-uk-offering-banned-treatments-derived-human-cells **[S]**; https://www.saveface.co.uk/en/blog/post/exosome-therapy-uk-patient-safety-warning **[S]**. I did not find an MHRA-authored page stating this: **MHRA primary statement not verified.**
- **PRP** is a blood component, outside the HTA Q&S regime, generally treated as non-ATMP; it is not a "stem cell" treatment and must not be advertised as one **[R]**.

**Two routes for unlicensed ATMPs.** "There are 2 ways in which unlicensed ATMPs can be made available in the UK: hospital exemption [and] the 'specials' scheme." (MHRA page above **[V]**.) From MHRA's *Guidance on the UK's hospital exemption scheme for ATMPs* **[V]** https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67c9ca8aae2aa47d2f5ae3e6/26_a_Guidance_on_the_UK_s_arrangements_under_the_hospital_exemption_scheme_clean__1_.pdf :

| | Hospital exemption | Specials |
|---|---|---|
| Who orders | "must be commissioned by a medical practitioner" | doctors, dentists, supplementary prescribers |
| Test | "custom made to meet an individual prescription and preparation must be on a 'non-routine basis'" | "special needs test (interpreted to mean the absence of a pharmaceutically equivalent and available licensed product)" |
| Where used | "must be used in a hospital" | no stipulation |
| Origin | made and used in the UK; "Import or export of exempt ATMPs is not permitted" | UK-made or imported (with MHRA import notification) |
| Licence | "A manufacturer is required to obtain a manufacturer's licence from the MHRA"; GMP; MHRA inspection; annual return; 30-year traceability | MHRA Manufacturer's "Specials" licence (GN14) |
| Advertising | "will not be permitted to advertise specific ATMPs made and used under the exemption... permissible to advertise the service that is provided" | GN14: unlicensed medicines must not be advertised (earlier report) |

Breach "could be liable to sanctions on the basis of placing a relevant medicinal product on the market without a marketing authorisation" **[V]**. A "special need" is a clinical need of an individual patient; MHRA GN14 excludes cost and convenience (see earlier report). A routine, menu-priced "longevity stem cell infusion" is difficult to reconcile with either "non-routine" or "special clinical need".

**Human Tissue Authority.** MHRA's hospital-exemption guidance states: "Under the Human Tissue (Quality and Safety for Human Application) Regulations 2007, the following activities must be carried out under the authority of an HTA licence: consent, donor selection, donor testing, procurement (collection), processing, storage, distribution and import and export. In the case of ATMPs the HTA regulates the donation (i.e. consent, donor selection and testing), procurement and testing of tissues and cells used in the manufacture of ATMPs. The manufacturing process following procurement... is regulated by the MHRA." **[V]** Regulation 7 of the 2007 Regulations: "No person shall store tissue or cells intended for human application otherwise than under the authority of a licence"; procurement, testing, processing, distribution, import and export need a licence or a third-party agreement with a licence holder. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/1523/regulation/7 **[V]**. So a clinic that **harvests fat or bone marrow** for a cell product is doing licensable *procurement* unless it works under a third-party agreement with an HTA-licensed establishment. The "autologous graft within the same surgical procedure" exclusion exists (HTA guide **[S]** https://www.hta.gov.uk/guidance-professionals/regulated-sectors/human-application/hta-guide-quality-and-safety-assurance) but does not rescue a product that is an ATMP. HTA pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch today, so HTA wording is [S], not [V].

**Scotland.** The Human Tissue Act 2004 does not apply in Scotland (the Human Tissue (Scotland) Act 2006 does), but the **Q&S Regulations 2007 are UK-wide** and the HTA licenses establishments handling tissue for human application in Scotland on behalf of Scottish Ministers https://www.hta.gov.uk/guidance-professionals/codes-practice-standards-and-legislation/legislation **[S]**; legislation.gov.uk reg. 7 **[V]**. Medicines law (HMR 2012, MHRA) is reserved and identical. Differences for Glasgow: the service regulator is **Healthcare Improvement Scotland** (independent clinic registration) rather than CQC; and the new **Non-surgical Procedures and Functions of Medical Reviewers (Scotland) Act 2026** lists "injectable procedure" and "intravenous procedure" in Schedule 1 and restricts them to "permitted premises" https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2026/13/contents **[V - contents only; Royal Assent date, commencement and the precise definition of permitted premises not verified]**.

**What a private clinic can lawfully offer (summary).**
- Refer to, or host, an MHRA-authorised clinical trial.
- Administer an unlicensed ATMP that was made by an MHRA-licensed manufacturer under hospital exemption (in a hospital) or as a special, prescribed by a doctor for an individual patient's special clinical need, with HTA-licensed procurement and full traceability.
- Offer non-ATMP autologous procedures with homologous use (e.g. fat grafting, PRP) - which are not "stem cell therapy".
- Offer a **consultation** about regenerative options.
- It cannot lawfully buy imported "MSC" or "exosome" vials from an unlicensed distributor and infuse them, however they are labelled.

**GMC.** The unlicensed-medicines duties in the earlier report apply with more force: necessity for the individual patient, sufficient evidence/experience of safety and efficacy, responsibility for follow-up, telling the patient it is unlicensed https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/good-practice-in-prescribing-and-managing-medicines-and-devices/prescribing-unlicensed-medicines **[V in earlier report]**. Precedent: Dr Robert Trossel was erased by the GMC in 2010 for "pointless" and "unjustified" stem cell treatments for MS patients https://www.progress.org.uk/stem-cell-doctor-struck-off-by-gmc/ **[S]**; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20884707/ **[S]**. I did not find a GMC position statement specific to stem cells: **not verified / none found.**

### A2. Advertising

- **HMR 2012 reg. 279**: "A person may not publish an advertisement in Great Britain for a medicinal product unless one of the following is in force for the product - (a) a UKMA(GB) or UKMA(UK)..." https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/1916/regulation/279 **[V]**. An unlicensed cell product or injectable exosome has none. This is a criminal-law prohibition, not just a code rule.
- **MHRA Blue Guide Appendix 6** (guidance for providers offering medicinal treatment services, Nov 2020): "Unlicensed medicines should not be mentioned, to comply with regulation 279... Treatments that involve the use of unlicensed medicines may not be described as 'clinically proven' or similar." Clinics "may promote the service they provide (e.g. medical consultation for individuals with...)". Enforcement focuses on home pages and social media; "Clinics and individual healthcare professionals may also be referred to their professional regulators". https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6012d8c9d3bf7f05c2040b4e/Appendix_6.pdf **[V]**
- MHRA publishes monthly lists of clinics made to amend sites (e.g. January 2026: Silva Wellness Ltd; SMS Healthcare Ltd t/a The Burlington Clinic - products not specified) https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advertising-investigations-january-2026/websites-offering-medicinal-treatment-services-january-2026 **[V]**.
- **CAP Code**: 12.1 (objective medical claims need evidence), 12.11 (medicines must be licensed before marketing), 12.12 (no POM advertising), 3.1/3.7, 12.2 (do not discourage essential treatment) - see earlier report for links.
- **ASA rulings.** I found **no ASA ruling against a UK stem cell clinic**, despite several searches of asa.org.uk and the literature. An academic survey of the UK market likewise reports no ASA/MHRA enforcement cases. The closest live ruling: **111 Skin Ltd, 13 May 2026, upheld**, "Exosome Face Lift... Clinically proven To Lift The Skin by 20%" - 30-person uncontrolled study inadequate; breaches 3.1, 3.7, 3.11, 12.1, 12.7 https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/111-skin-ltd-a25-1319497-111-skin-ltd.html **[V]**. That is a topical cosmetic; an injected exosome would face the stricter medicinal rules.
- **Platforms.** *Google Ads* bans "speculative and experimental medical treatment", expressly including stem cell therapy, cellular therapy, gene therapy and PRP, with narrow exceptions https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15596627 **[S]**. *Meta*: I read Meta's Health & Wellness ad standard today and it **does not mention stem cells, exosomes, regenerative medicine, peptides or genetic tests** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/ **[V]**. So Meta has no stem-cell-specific rule that I could verify; the binding constraints on Meta are its cure-claim, clickbait/timeframe, personal-attributes and prescription-drug rules (earlier report) plus UK law. Third-party claims that Meta needs LegitScript for stem cell ads are **not verified**.

**How the law bites.**
- MHRA to the press, April 2026: "Peptide products may be sold as cosmetics, supplements and medicines, and depending on their intended purpose, they fall under different regulatory frameworks... If there is evidence within the promotional material that the products are in fact unauthorised medicines intended for human use, we will take appropriate regulatory action." MHRA disregards "research purposes" disclaimers where human use is evident. Triggered by a Guardian investigation (4 April 2026) that found a clinic listing BPC-157, thymosin alpha, Cortexin and MOTS-c with benefits and prices; claims were removed after contact. https://www.pharmacy.biz/mhra-peptide-injections-investigation-uk/ **[V - secondary]**; https://aestheticsjournal.com/news/mhra-investigates-clinics-following-peptide-claims/ **[S]**. I could not find an MHRA-authored press release on peptides on gov.uk: **primary source not verified.**
- MHRA has pursued melanotan II for over a decade: "The sale and advertising of these products is illegal" (MHRA FOI 21/1237) https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67bc97a898ea2db44fadddb1/FOI_21-1237_.pdf **[S]**. Grey-market vendor blogs claim Operation Pangea 2025 named BPC-157 and ipamorelin: **not verified.**
- Injectable medicines are prescription-only by the HMR 2012 criteria (earlier report, [R]); a clinic can only administer an unlicensed peptide on a doctor's patient-specific prescription, sourced from an **MHRA-licensed specials manufacturer or licensed importer** - not from a "research chemical" website. A vial labelled "not for human use" has no place in a CQC/HIS-registered clinic.
- **GMC**: same unlicensed-prescribing standard as A1. With no human efficacy data, "sufficient evidence or experience of using the medicine to demonstrate its safety and efficacy" is very hard to satisfy for BPC-157, TB-500, epitalon or MOTS-c.
- **WADA**: all at-all-times prohibitions. Any tested athlete must be warned. Official list: https://www.wada-ama.org/en/prohibited-list **[page did not render today; section numbers above are from secondary summaries - S]**.

### B2. ASA position on GLP-1s and what it implies for peptides

CAP AdviceOnline "Weight control: Prescription-only medicines", updated **29 May 2026** https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/weight-control-prescription-only-medicines.html **[V]**:
- Treated as POM references: "weight loss injections", "skinny jab", "weight loss pen", **"GLP-1"**, "Obesity Treatment Jab", branded **and unbranded** pen images, vial images, "from £139.50 per pen", "ONCE-A-WEEK" with weight-loss claims; even "weight loss treatments" when the landing page implies a POM.
- Ads may offer "a consultation for weight loss" but must not signal that the likely outcome is a POM, including via the landing page.
- Narrow allowance for **inner pages of the clinic's own website (not the homepage)**.
- Rulings: Chequp Health (9 Jul 2025; 17 Dec 2025), Juniper Technologies (9 Jul 2025; 8 Apr 2026), SemaPen (9 Jul 2025), Menwell (27 May 2026); the July 2025 batch also covered pharmacyonline.co.uk, HealthExpress, Cloud Pharmacy, Phlo Clinic and Yazen **[S for the full list]**. CAP enforcement report: ~900 ads from 38 advertisers assessed as likely breaches https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/enforcement-report-weight-loss-prescription-only-medicines.html **[S]**.
- **No ASA ruling on "peptide therapy" found.** By analogy, "peptide therapy", "peptides", "GH secretagogue", "recovery peptides" and vial imagery would be treated as references to unlicensed medicines/POMs.

### C2. Reliability

- Technical noise in first/second-generation clocks up to ~9 years between replicates; PC clocks within ~1.5 years (Higgins-Chen 2022 - earlier report).
- **New (2026): Sehgal et al., *Aging Cell*, "Biological versus technical reliability of epigenetic clocks"** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418614/ **[V]**. Technical ICC > 0.9 for most modern clocks (SystemsAge, PC clocks), ~0.7-0.8 for original GrimAge/Hannum/PhenoAge. But **biological (same person, hours-to-days apart) ICCs: no clock reached > 0.90; SystemsAge, PCGrimAge, OMICmAge, PCPhenoAge ~0.6-0.7; GrimAge, DunedinPACE, DNAmEMRAge ~0.4-0.5.** Meals, acute stress, pollution and time of day moved the clocks. "Technical reliability did not predict biological reliability" (r = 0.0168). Low-reliability clocks showed intervention effects "often flipping direction" across resamples. Authors advise controlling fasting status, time of day and stress at both draws and prioritising PC-based/SystemsAge clocks for longitudinal tracking. Two authors declare TruDiagnostic consulting fees. No minimum-detectable-change figure is given.
- **COSMOS-Blood, *GeroScience* 2025** (n=899, mean age 70, 2 years) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13575030 **[V]**: 2-year ICCs 0.82-0.94, PC versions higher (PC Horvath 0.94 vs 0.85); PC clocks cut variance ~50-65%; PC clocks advanced only ~0.14-0.20 "years" per calendar year; DunedinPACE showed no significant change; detecting a 0.20-year effect needs ~1,270 participants per arm.
- DunedinPACE technical ICC 0.96-0.97 (Belsky 2022 - earlier report) is a *same-sample* figure; the Sehgal data show its day-to-day biological stability is much lower.

**Most defensible metric for before/after tracking:** a **PC-based clock (PCGrimAge / PCPhenoAge) or SystemsAge-family measure** as the headline, with DunedinPACE shown as a secondary pace measure, *not* a single first-generation "biological age". Report direction and magnitude with an explicit uncertainty statement.

**Protocol to make a retest meaningful:** same lab, same sample type (prefer venous/finger-prick blood over saliva), fasted, same time of day (morning), no acute illness, vaccination, surgery, heavy training or long-haul travel in the prior 2-3 weeks (Poganik 2023 - earlier report), record smoking/alcohol changes. **Minimum sensible retest interval: 6 months; 12 months preferred.** Shorter intervals mostly measure noise. No study supports monthly or quarterly retesting of individuals; this interval is a judgement from the trial literature (CALERIE 12/24 months; DO-HEALTH 3 years), not a validated threshold.

### C4. Honest positioning and "reversal" claims

**Positioning.** The free quiz is a *questionnaire-based estimate built from population risk factors*. The lab test is a *measurement of molecular markers that, in population studies, track morbidity and mortality risk*. Neither is a diagnosis; neither is "your true age". The bridge is: "The questionnaire tells you which habits are probably helping or hurting. A blood-based test gives a measured baseline that can be repeated." Do not imply that the quiz predicts the lab result - the clinic has no data showing that it does; if it collects paired data, it could honestly report the correlation later.

**Defensible on retest:**
- "Your PC-GrimAge moved from X to Y between [date] and [date]. Changes of this size can occur from measurement and day-to-day biological variation alone; the trend over several tests, together with your blood pressure, HbA1c, lipids, waist and fitness, is more informative than any single pair."
- "In randomised trials, calorie restriction slowed DunedinPACE by about 2-3% (CALERIE), and vitamin D/omega-3/exercise produced small changes over 3 years (DO-HEALTH)" - i.e. real effects are small (earlier report).

**Not defensible:**
- "You reversed your biological age by 4 years" as a causal claim from one before/after pair.
- "Our [drip/EBOO/peptide/stem cell] programme reduces biological age" - no RCT for any of them; and it converts the test into a tool "to monitor therapeutic measures" (IVD territory) and an efficacy claim for an unlicensed medicine.
- Aggregate marketing claims ("patients average 3.2 years younger") without a controlled, pre-registered analysis: regression to the mean, selective retesting and noise guarantee an apparent improvement in people who test "old" at baseline.
- Testimonials with numbers ("I'm now 38 biologically!") - CAP treats testimonial claims as the advertiser's claims (substantiation still required).

- **Genetic/epigenetic test content (Muhdo):** the chatbot may say the clinic offers a saliva-based epigenetic age estimate and DNA wellbeing profile, that it is a wellness test and not a diagnosis, and that a doctor reviews results. **Suggested wording.**
- *Medicine or peptide named:* "I'm not able to discuss specific medicines or injectable treatments here - UK rules don't allow clinics to promote them, and whether any treatment is right for you can only be decided by a doctor who has assessed you. I can help you book a consultation with one of our GMC-registered doctors, where you can ask about any option."

Implementation: enforce with a server-side blocklist + system prompt, log deflections, never let the model generate treatment names in responses, and do not pass chat content to Meta/analytics.

### D3. Recommended public wording

**If the test is Muhdo (current vendor) - use this version, not the blood-based text below:**

> **Epigenetic age estimate (saliva).** A saliva test analysed by Muhdo, a UK company, that estimates age from DNA-methylation patterns. It is a **wellness test, not a medical diagnosis**. The estimate typically differs from a person's calendar age by around five years in either direction, so a single result - or a small change between two results - should not be over-interpreted. We use it as one data point alongside standard clinical measurements (blood pressure, blood tests, body composition, fitness), which remain the basis for any medical advice. Results are reviewed with a GMC-registered doctor. Not offered to under-18s. Your sample and results are sensitive personal data; we ask for explicit consent and explain who processes them and where.

Do not use: "gold standard", "peer reviewed" without qualification, "precise", "real-time", "99.99% accurate", "PACE", or any organ-age, disease or trait item.

**Epigenetic test - generic text for a blood-based array test (if the clinic adds one):**

> **Epigenetic age testing.** A laboratory analysis of DNA-methylation patterns in a small blood sample. It reports research-derived measures of biological ageing, including pace-of-ageing (DunedinPACE) and principal-component epigenetic clocks. In large population studies these measures are associated with health outcomes; they are **not a diagnosis, do not predict what will happen to you as an individual, and are not a substitute for standard checks** such as blood pressure, cholesterol and blood-sugar tests.
> Results vary with measurement and day-to-day factors, so we test in the morning, fasted, and recommend **re-testing no sooner than 6-12 months**. A change between two tests does not by itself show that any treatment worked. Your result is reviewed with you by a GMC-registered doctor.
> Your sample is analysed by [lab, country]. It is sensitive personal data: we ask for your explicit consent, send the lab a coded ID rather than your name, and explain how your data is stored and when your sample is destroyed. If you plan to apply for life or health insurance, please read our note on test results and insurance first.

**Quiz-result bridge:** "Your result is an estimate from a lifestyle and health questionnaire - not a laboratory measurement. If you'd like a measured baseline, our doctors can arrange blood tests and, if you choose, an epigenetic age test, and explain what they can and can't tell you."

**Ad-safe line:** "Questionnaire estimate first. Lab-measured markers if you want them. Reviewed by a doctor." Avoid: "true age", "real age", "reverse", "turn back", "X years younger", "clinically proven", "most accurate".

**"Regenerative medicine consultations".** Permissible only as a *service* description with no product, source, mechanism, condition or outcome: 

> "Our doctors offer consultations on regenerative and musculoskeletal medicine, including an evidence review of options available in the UK and through clinical trials."

No "stem cell", "exosome", "MSC", "cellular rejuvenation", "anti-ageing", before/afters or testimonials. Not in paid ads (Google bans the category; on Meta it invites scrutiny for no benefit). If the clinic cannot answer the D4 questions affirmatively, my recommendation is to **omit this line altogether** - advertising a consultation whose likely outcome is an unlawfully supplied product is not cured by careful wording. Get CAP Copy Advice and consider writing to advertising@mhra.gov.uk before publishing.

### D4. Questions for the owner to confirm

**Stem cells / exosomes**
1. What exactly is the product (autologous/allogeneic; adipose, marrow, cord, Wharton's jelly; expanded or not; exosome/secretome), who manufactures it, and in which country?
2. Does the manufacturer hold an **MHRA manufacturer's licence** covering ATMPs under hospital exemption (MeAT-type) or a **Manufacturer's Specials (MS) licence**? If imported, which licensed importer submitted the **MHRA import notification**, and is there an **HTA import licence**?
3. Has the product's classification (ATMP or not) been confirmed in writing by the **MHRA Innovation Office / Borderline Section**?
4. If tissue is harvested on site: does the clinic hold an **HTA human-application licence** for procurement (and storage/processing if applicable), or a **third-party agreement** with an HTA-licensed establishment? Who is the Designated Individual?
5. If relying on hospital exemption: is administration taking place in a **hospital**, on a non-routine, individually prescribed basis?
6. Is each treatment prescribed by a named GMC-registered doctor after in-person assessment, with documented unlicensed-medicine consent, donor-to-recipient **traceability (30 years)**, and Yellow Card / serious adverse reaction reporting?
7. Does the medical indemnity policy expressly cover unlicensed cell-based therapy?

**Peptides / GLP-1s**
8. Are all peptides obtained from an **MHRA-licensed specials manufacturer or licensed wholesaler/importer**, with certificates of analysis - and none from "research use only" suppliers?
9. Is there a written prescribing policy meeting GMC unlicensed-medicines guidance, with a recorded clinical rationale per patient?
10. Are GLP-1s supplied only as licensed products via a GPhC-registered pharmacy, within or knowingly outside licensed indications?
11. Is there a **Home Office controlled-drugs licence** if any controlled drug (e.g. testosterone, Schedule 4) is stocked at a non-exempt site? (Requirement depends on setting - not verified.)

**Service regulators**
12. London: does the **CQC registration** list the regulated activities actually performed - *Treatment of disease, disorder or injury*; *Diagnostic and screening procedures* (venepuncture/blood analysis); *Surgical procedures* if liposuction or marrow aspiration is done - and does the **statement of purpose** name EBOO, IV therapy and any regenerative procedures?

14. Are both sites' names in advertising identical to the registered provider names (ASA and CQC both check this)?

**Epigenetic testing / data**

Is there a **data processing (or controller-to-controller) agreement**, a **DPIA**, **ICO registration**, an Article 9 condition documented, and a lawful **transfer mechanism** (UK Extension certification or IDTA + TRA)? Does the lab reuse data for research, and can patients opt out? When are samples destroyed?
