## 1. Mechanism of action

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is a coenzyme present in every nucleated cell and operates at the intersection of energy metabolism and cellular repair. In its oxidised (NAD+) and reduced (NADH) forms it acts as the principal electron carrier feeding Complex I of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, driving oxidative phosphorylation and ATP production (Source: Verdin, *Science*, 2015). Beyond bioenergetics, NAD+ is consumed as a substrate by three enzyme families: **sirtuins** (SIRT1-7, which deacetylate histones and metabolic regulators including PGC-1α and FOXO3, supporting mitochondrial biogenesis and stress resistance), **PARPs** (poly-ADP-ribose polymerases, which detect and repair single- and double-strand DNA breaks), and **CD38/CD157** (immune-associated NAD+ hydrolases that rise sharply with age) (Source: Camacho-Pereira et al., *Cell Metabolism*, 2016).

Tissue NAD+ concentrations decline roughly 10-65% between ages 30 and 70 depending on tissue type, driven primarily by rising CD38 activity and increased PARP demand from accumulated DNA damage (Source: Massudi et al., *PLoS ONE*, 2012; Gomes et al., *Cell*, 2013).

The clinical hypothesis behind IV NAD+ is that bolus repletion bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism and gut breakdown that limit oral NAD+ (oral NAD+ itself is largely degraded to nicotinamide before absorption). However, even IV NAD+ does not enter cells intact — it is hydrolysed extracellularly to nicotinamide and NMN/NR precursors before transport (Source: Liu et al., *Cell Metabolism*, 2018, isotope-tracer work).

**Honest framing for copywriting:** IV NAD+ delivers a large precursor load efficiently, but the "direct intracellular flooding" narrative oversells the cell biology.

## 2. Evidence-based benefits

### (a) Reasonable human evidence or strong mechanistic support

- **Raises whole-blood / intracellular NAD+ metabolome.** Oral NR at 250-1000 mg/day reliably elevates blood NAD+ by 40-90% in placebo-controlled RCTs (Martens et al., *Nature Communications*, 2018; Conze et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2019). IV NAD+ pharmacokinetics characterised in small open-label study (Grant et al., *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*, 2019, n=11).
- **Modest improvements in mitochondrial / muscle markers in older adults.** NR improved muscle bioenergetic markers and reduced IL-6, IL-2, TNF-α in adults 55-79 (Martens 2018; Elhassan et al., *Cell Reports*, 2019).
- **Parkinson's exploratory signal.** NADPARK trial: oral NR 1g/day, 30 days, measurable brain NAD+ elevation + mild motor improvement (Brakedal et al., *Cell Metabolism*, 2022).
- **Metabolic markers in prediabetic/obese.** NMN 250 mg/day improved muscle insulin sensitivity in postmenopausal women with prediabetes (Yoshino et al., *Science*, 2021).
- **DNA-damage / PARP biology.** Strong preclinical case that NAD+ availability is rate-limiting for PARP-mediated DNA repair (Fang et al., *Cell Metabolism*, 2016).

### (b) Commonly marketed but weak / anecdotal

- "Reverses biological age" / lowers epigenetic clock age — **no published human RCT** for IV NAD+
- Addiction recovery — case series only, no published RCT to modern standards
- Chronic fatigue / long COVID cure — small open-label pilots only
- Cognitive enhancement in healthy adults — extrapolated from rodent work
- "Detoxification" — no mechanistic basis as marketed
- Athletic performance / VO2 max gains — mixed, mostly null in human data

**Defensible copy claims:** "supports cellular energy production," "supports the body's NAD+-dependent repair processes," "may support recovery from periods of high physical or cognitive demand." Anything stronger requires HSW's medical director to sign off on a held-evidence file.

## 3. Ideal candidate symptom profile (quiz scoring inputs)

Score should up-weight when **multiple co-present**, not single symptoms:

- **Post-exertional / "wired-but-tired" fatigue** — normal/long sleep but unrefreshing; afternoon energy crashes; mental fatigue disproportionate to physical activity; reduced exercise tolerance over last 12-24 months
- **High allostatic load profile** — chronic work stress >50h/week, frequent international travel/jetlag, late-night cognitive work, alcohol use 10+ units/week
- **Subjective cognitive load symptoms** — "brain fog," word-finding difficulty under fatigue, reduced focus stamina (NOT memory loss — that warrants neurology referral)
- **Slow recovery markers** — DOMS >72h, frequent minor illness, slow wound healing, declining gym performance
- **Mitochondrial-stress proxies** — heavy alcohol history, prior chemotherapy, statin myopathy symptoms, long-term metformin, known mitochondrial disorder
- **Age 40+ with declining biomarkers** — rising fasting glucose/HbA1c, rising hs-CRP, declining VO2 max
- **Sleep architecture disruption** — perimenopausal sleep fragmentation, shift work

### Disqualifying / refer-out signals (route to GP, NOT booking)

- Unexplained weight loss
- Night sweats
- Lymphadenopathy
- New severe headaches
- Depressive symptoms with anhedonia >2 weeks
- Chest pain on exertion

These are red flags for malignancy, depression, or cardiac disease and must not be treated as "low NAD+."

## 4. Contraindications & screening exclusions

### Absolute contraindications (do not infuse)

- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (no safety data)
- **Active malignancy or cancer treatment within last 5 years** — NAD+ supports DNA repair *and* cellular proliferation; theoretical concern about supporting tumour metabolism. **Single biggest medico-legal exclusion.**
- Active severe infection / sepsis
- Known hypersensitivity to NAD+ or infusion components
- Severe hepatic or renal impairment (Child-Pugh C, eGFR <30)

### Relative contraindications / require physician review

- Cardiovascular disease, recent MI, unstable angina, uncontrolled hypertension
- Bipolar disorder or active psychosis
- Concurrent chemotherapy, PARP inhibitors, or immunosuppression
- Anticoagulation
- History of vasovagal syncope

### Standard pre-screen

Capture: full medical history, current medications, pregnancy status (urine HCG day of infusion for women of childbearing age), allergies, prior IV reactions, baseline bloods (FBC, U&E, LFT, HbA1c, lipids). Many Harley Street clinics upsell a "longevity panel" here. **Face-to-face or video consultation with a prescriber (GMC-registered doctor or independent nurse prescriber) before first infusion** is the MHRA-defensible standard for an unlicensed indication.

### Common infusion-related side effects (counsel & document)

- Chest tightness / pressure (30-60% of patients, dose-rate dependent, resolves on slowing drip)
- Flushing, warmth, head pressure
- Nausea, abdominal cramping
- Muscle tightness in jaw/legs
- Anxiety / "air hunger" sensation
- Headache during or post-infusion
- Bruising/phlebitis at cannulation site

These are rate-related and almost always resolve by slowing the drip — this is why infusions run 2-4 hours rather than 30 minutes.

## 5. Typical UK clinic protocol

**Per-session dose:** UK private clinics: 500-1000 mg standard, 1000-1500 mg "high-dose/loading." Harley Street norms sit at the lower end for tolerability and medico-legal conservatism.

**Session duration:** 2-4 hours standard. 500 mg ~2 hours, 1000 mg 3-4 hours. Pump rates titrated down on symptoms.

**Course recommendations:**
- **Single trial drip** (250-500 mg, ~90 min) — appropriate for £295 intro offer
- **Standard course:** 3-5 drips of 500-750 mg over 2-3 weeks (loading), then maintenance every 4-12 weeks
- **Intensive longevity protocol:** 5-10 consecutive daily drips at 750-1000 mg (more common in US "BR+NAD")

**Timeline to feel effects:** Subjective energy/clarity within 24-72 hours of first infusion, cumulative over 3-5 drip course. Most describe "feeling like themselves again" — not stimulant-like. Effects from single drip wane over 2-6 weeks — commercial basis for maintenance bookings.

**At £295:** almost certainly a single 250-500 mg "intro/boost" drip. Full 750-1000 mg sessions typically £395-£595 in Harley Street; 5-drip courses £1,500-£2,500.

## 6. UK ASA / MHRA compliance — claim mapping

Regulatory frame: CAP Code (non-broadcast) administered by the ASA + Human Medicines Regulations 2012 enforced by the MHRA. NAD+ is **not a licensed medicine in the UK**; it is administered as unlicensed product under named-patient/specials route by a prescribing clinician.

### Claims to AVOID and safer alternatives

| Avoid | Why | Safer alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Reverses biological age" | Unsubstantiated, mouse-data extrapolation | "Supports the body's natural cellular repair processes" |
| "Anti-aging treatment" (unqualified) | ASA treats this as medical claim requiring trial evidence | "A wellness protocol used by clients focused on healthy ageing" |
| "Cures fatigue / depression / addiction" | "Cure" is a medicinal claim — MHRA territory | "May support energy levels and mental clarity during demanding periods" |
| "Boosts your NAD+ levels by X%" with a number | Requires per-patient trial evidence | "Designed to support cellular NAD+ availability" |
| "Detoxifies the body" | ASA has ruled "detox" misleading | **Omit. No safe version.** |
| "Proven to" / "clinically proven" | Requires UK-quality RCT in the claimed indication | "Supported by emerging research into NAD+ biology" |
| "Treats long COVID / CFS / Parkinson's" | Medicinal claim for a serious condition — MHRA breach | **Do not mention these conditions in public copy.** |
| Before/after testimonials referencing condition resolution | ASA prohibits condition-referencing testimonials | Experiential only ("I felt clearer after my session") — no condition names |
| Targeting "serious medical conditions" | CAP Code Section 12 restriction | Position as wellness/lifestyle, not therapy |

### Additional MHRA traps

- Do not describe NAD+ as a "medicine" or "treatment" in public copy
- Do not name competitor drugs (e.g. "alternative to antidepressants")
- Named-patient/specials route requires individual prescription per patient — copy must make clear booking is contingent on consultation
- Price advertising of prescription-only intervention to public is permitted but must not "induce" use — frame as consultation-led care

### Defensible umbrella language

> "A wellness infusion protocol supporting cellular energy, cognitive performance, and recovery, offered following a private medical consultation with our prescribing clinicians."

### Page requirements

- GMC-registered prescriber named on the page
- CQC registration visible (NAD+ IV in a clinic setting falls under CQC scope)
- Consent form references NAD+ as unlicensed product administered under clinician prescription
- No health claims on paid social/Google without held evidence file
- Influencer content carries #ad, pre-vetted against CAP Code

## 7. Honest assessment for HSW

**For the burned-out executive (35-55):** NAD+ IV is genuinely defensible as a high-touch wellness intervention. This cohort presents with allostatic-load fatigue, alcohol-load, sleep debt, and elevated PARP/CD38 demand — exactly the substrate where precursor repletion is most plausible. The subjective "back to myself" response is real and reproducible enough that repeat-purchase economics work without overclaiming.

**For the HNW anti-aging buyer (45-65):** Picture is more honest-mixed. Credible mechanistic story (NAD+ decline is real, sirtuin/PARP biology is real) but **no human trial demonstrating that IV NAD+ extends lifespan or reverses epigenetic age in people**. This persona is buying a longevity narrative. HSW can serve them ethically — only if copy stays on "support cellular health during ageing" ground rather than promising age reversal.

**Where NAD+ IV is overhyped:** as a standalone longevity intervention (one input into a sleep/exercise/metabolic-health stack, not a substitute), as a "cure" for any named condition, as a one-shot fix (the £295 single drip should be honestly positioned as a trial/experience, with measurable outcomes coming from courses + lifestyle work).

**Defensible commercial position:** "premium concierge wellness with a real biochemical rationale" — not "anti-aging medicine."
