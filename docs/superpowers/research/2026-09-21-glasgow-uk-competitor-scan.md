### 4. HIS vs CQC — what it means for HSW
- England: CQC registers providers of regulated activities in England. Scotland: HIS regulates independent hospitals, independent clinics and (since 2025) independent medical agencies under the National Health Service (Scotland) Act 1978. CQC registration confers nothing in Scotland, and vice versa. Get A Drip's Edinburgh "coming soon" page still cites CQC only; ESTE's site admits only its Birmingham branch is CQC registered.
- An "independent clinic" is premises in Scotland where doctors/nurses/pharmacists etc. provide private face-to-face services. If HSW ever treats in Scotland (pop-up, room rental, mobile IV nurse), it needs HIS registration first; HIS says services may not trade before registration and quotes processing times of roughly three to six months. Providing an unregistered service is an offence (search results cite s.10Z9 of the 1978 Act — section number not verified on legislation.gov.uk).
- Online-only services are now treated by HIS as Independent Medical Agencies, BUT HIS's own guidance (Scenario 5) says a prescribing clinician whose "legal entity that is the provider of the service is based in England" is not subject to HIS regulation. So an England-based, CQC-registered HSW offering a remote free consultation to a Scottish resident does not, on HIS's published guidance, need HIS registration. https://www.healthcareimprovementscotland.scot/inspections-reviews-and-regulation/regulation-of-independent-healthcare/independent-healthcare-find-out-if-you-need-to-register/information-for-service-providers-independent-medical-agency/ . Get this confirmed in writing (his.ihcregulation@nhs.scot) — this is a reading of guidance, not legal advice.
- Advertising: the ASA/CAP Code is UK-wide, so the Dec 2023 IV-drip rulings (no health-benefit claims for IV drips in healthy people without evidence; no medicinal claims for unlicensed products; prescription-only medicines cannot be advertised to the public) apply identically to ads served in Glasgow.
- Commercial reality: an IV drip cannot be delivered remotely. Advertising to Glasgow without a Scottish site means asking leads to travel about 400 miles for a £295 drip, against a £275 local option. Scotland only makes sense for (a) high-ticket items with no local supply (EBOO £1,995 — one Glasgow rival; methylene blue / phospholipid exchange — none found), (b) remote programmes (metabolic health), or (c) as demand-testing ahead of an HIS-registered site or partner. Ads and the quiz report must not imply a Scottish clinic or HIS registration, and should say treatment is in London.
